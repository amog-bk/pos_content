Insurance this week

The headline in the insurance sector this week is Prudential's massive
₹3,500 crore acquisition of a 75% stake in Bharti Life Insurance. In
parallel, Indian non-life insurance sectors are seeing surging retail
health premiums, which grew 31% year-on-year. Meanwhile, the Insurance
Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) is cracking down on
corporate governance by withholding the variable pay of multiple insurance
CEOs.

Major Business & Market Deals

Prudential's Strategic Acquisition: Global giant Prudential agreed to buy a
75% stake in Bharti Life Insurance for ₹3,500 crore, with an additional
₹700 crore contingent on milestones.

ICICI Pru Rebalancing: To comply with rules and manage this massive
transition, Prudential will pare its 12% stake in ICICI Prudential Life
Insurance over the next 12 to 18 months.

Tata AIA Bonus Growth: Demonstrating robust financial health, Tata AIA Life
raised its bonus payout to participating policyholders by 18%, totaling
$217 million.

IndusInd Nippon Profit Jump: IndusInd Nippon Life posted a 15% profit surge
for FY2026 and announced plans to open 200 new sales units across India.

Regulatory Actions & Corporate Governance

CEO Pay Withheld: The IRDAI withheld variable pay for certain insurance
CEOs due to a failure to meet 'expense of management' targets.

Tightening Performance Metrics: Life insurance companies are actively
resisting a push by the regulator to tie executive pay directly to strict
Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) involving claims settlement and customer
service.

Investment Easing: To help diversify portfolios, the IRDAI is considering
easing norms to allow insurers to invest heavier capital into Real Estate
Investment Trusts (REITs) and Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs).

Industry & Premium Trends

Health Insurance Booms: Retail health insurance remains the engine of
growth for the non-life segment, posting a 31% YoY growth according to
Kotak Institutional Securities.

Tier-2 & Tier-3 Discounts: Star Health and Allied Insurance rolled out
targeted healthcare products that are 20% cheaper specifically for smaller
cities.

High Operational Costs: A report by Praxis Global Alliance highlighted that
general insurance costs remain artificially elevated due to heavy
intermediary commissions on renewal businesses.
