*Insurance this week*

IRDAI is pressing on the cost side from three angles this week — withholding CEO variable pay over EoM breaches, tying half of senior assessment to customer outcomes, and reworking broker-commission rules. DIPAM, meanwhile, lines up a ₹10,000-crore LIC stake sale and PE firms eye IndusInd General Insurance.

*Major Business & Market Deals*

_100% FDI Notified:_ The May 2 FEMA NDI amendment allows 100% FDI in insurance via the automatic route, with intermediaries included. LIC stays capped at 20%, and insurers with foreign investment must keep a resident Indian as chair, MD or CEO.

_₹10,000-crore LIC Stake Sale:_ DIPAM begins formal marketing in late June or early July for a 1.5–2% LIC stake worth up to ₹10,000 crore (~$1 billion). Goldman Sachs, Motilal Oswal, BNP Paribas and IIFL Capital are on the deal; LIC shares fell ~4% on the report.

_PE Interest in IndusInd General Insurance:_ Moneycontrol reports Bain Capital and other private-equity firms are exploring stake buys in IIHL-promoted IndusInd General Insurance — the rebranded Reliance General the Hinduja entity took over through its Reliance Capital acquisition. IIHL plans to list the insurance arm within two years.

_LIC Bonus Record Date Today:_ LIC's first-ever 1:1 bonus share issue takes Friday, May 29 as its record date — shareholders on the books today receive one additional share for each held, marking the state insurer's maiden bonus issue since its 2022 listing.

*Regulatory Actions & Corporate Governance*

_Variable Pay Withheld:_ IRDAI has withheld the performance-linked pay of insurance CEOs whose companies missed Expense of Management targets or strayed from their EoM glide paths, after reviewing FY25 results and early FY26 trends.

_Pay Tied to Customer Outcomes:_ A revised KMP framework now links 50% of senior assessment — for MDs, CEOs and key personnel — to financial soundness, product performance, claims responsiveness, grievance redressal, Ind AS implementation and removing "dark patterns" in customer journeys. Effective FY27. Insurers have flagged regulatory-overreach concerns.

_Broker Commission Rules Revised:_ IRDAI has replaced individual-product commission caps with an aggregate, board-approved limit at the insurer level, folded into the EoM framework (proposed at 30% of GWP for general insurers, 35% for standalone health). The shift emphasises post-sale service and total-commission transparency for corporate clients.

_Uniform Claim Definition Coming:_ IRDAI has directed non-life insurers to agree on a single definition of "claim" and a uniform methodology for claim settlement ratios — today insurers count differently, and rejected or disputed claims sometimes still show up as "settled". The General Insurance Council has submitted its views.

_Health Moratorium Cut to Five Years:_ The period after which health claims cannot be rejected for non-disclosure or misrepresentation has been reduced from 8 years to 5 — a notable consumer-protection shift. Proven fraud and permanent policy exclusions remain valid rejection grounds even after the moratorium kicks in.

*Industry & Premium Trends*

_Niva Bupa Eyes Top Two:_ India's third-largest standalone health insurer reported FY26 GWP of ₹9,432.9 crore — retail health ~70% (₹6,581.5 crore), up 35% YoY. It now targets a 22–24% retail CAGR over the next 3–4 years, with high single-digit premium hikes likely as the claims ratio crept to 64.9%.

_Insurance Cyber-Fraud Cases Rise:_ Insurance-themed cyber-fraud cases surfaced across Navi Mumbai this week, including a Vashi resident duped of ₹61.49 lakh in a fake insurance-refund scam and an IT manager taken for ₹7 lakh via a bogus life-policy pitch — with police tracking organised rings spanning multiple cities.
